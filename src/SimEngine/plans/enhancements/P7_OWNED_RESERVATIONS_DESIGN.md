## 0. Problem statement

The initial audit found anonymous resource aggregates, release-before-physical-transfer windows and
InHeld/OutHeld booleans. C1 replaced those with owned endpoint records and job references. C2a removes
source/destination promises waiting on an unspecified carrier: the selected worker and cargo now
participate at acquisition. Tool/dock requirements and the generic transaction protocol are later work.

## 1. Goals

1. C1: replace anonymous promise mutation APIs and boolean job receipts in place with owned records.
2. C1: reject wrong requester, endpoint, direction, stale/double token and duplicate active commitment
   without changing stock/hash. Reserved pickup/admission consumes exactly its own token once.
3. C1: save/hash the same endpoint records and job references, with no aggregate balance cache.
4. C2: negotiate one exact quantity across source, destination and selected carrier; acquire endpoints,
   worker/cargo/tool participants all or none, using the existing WorkBoard/worker ownership.
5. C3: physical continuation, cancellation/despawn, malicious providers and transaction preview parity
   prove conservation/refusal through public contracts. Resource journals are facts, never balances.

## 2. Design

Core/ResourceReservations.cs declares immutable ReservationOwner(scope, identity), ReservationToken
(endpoint, sequence), ResourceReservation and the endpoint-embedded ResourceReservationBook. The book
owns reservation records only, replacing the old promise lists/quantities. Its derived inbound/outbound
vectors sum those records; no second aggregate balance is stored. Each endpoint retains its own stock.
Monotonic positive sequence IDs do not reuse released tokens. One owner/resource/direction may have
one active record; duplicates refuse before mutating the sequence. Owner scopes identify registered
engine responsibilities; these IDs are simulation identity, not authentication secrets.

Providers validate capacity/native precision before Reserve. Reserved Receive/Take validate the record,
requester, endpoint and direction, then atomically consume that record and change actual stock exactly.
Incoming validation omits only that owned record while retaining every other promise. No release/add
window remains for physical port delivery. Ordinary unreserved operations remain bound by promises.
The independent proof provider embeds the same record mechanism while retaining its own stock codec.

Job replaces InHeld/OutHeld with endpoint token references; its requester is scope sim.haul plus job ID.
Logistics preflights endpoint reservations before acquisition, saves references on that existing job,
consumes the owned pickup/admission on physical arrival and releases only remaining owned receipts.
Removed endpoints invalidate their own records; surviving participants release normally. A reserved
inventory refuses replacement until its owners end/release commitments; never copy/clear another
job's tokens onto a new entity identity. C1 alone does not reserve a selected carrier atomically.
