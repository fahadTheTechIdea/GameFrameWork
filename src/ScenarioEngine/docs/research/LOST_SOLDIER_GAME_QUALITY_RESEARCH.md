# What made comparable lost-soldier / behind-the-lines games good or bad?

Date: 24 October 2026. Status: comparative research; no design selected.

## Evidence snapshot

The released comparison set has a clear spread rather than a universal failure:

- [Commandos: Behind Enemy Lines](https://store.steampowered.com/app/6800/Commandos_Behind_Enemy_Lines/) currently shows 90% positive from 1,261 English Steam reviews. [GameSpot's contemporary review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/commandos-behind-enemy-lines-review/1900-2538454/) scored it 8.4/10.
- [Partisans 1941](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1227530/Partisans_1941/) currently shows 77% positive from 1,689 English Steam reviews. [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/partisans-1941-review/) scored it 80; another review recorded substantial frustration with fiddly controls, enemy behaviour and repetitive looting.
- [Broken Lines](https://store.steampowered.com/app/926580/Broken_Lines/) currently shows 76% positive from 605 Steam reviews and a 76 Metacritic critic score. Reviews identify strong character/choice ideas but weaker polish, pacing, tactical depth and mission variety.
- [OUTGUNNED - Behind Enemy Lines](https://www.dicesterstudio.com/outgunned/) and [Extraktion 1943](https://store.steamp.com/app/2631080/Extraktion_1943/) are not usable quality evidence: the available pages describe announced/in-development games, not a shipped player reception. Their premises are useful precedent evidence only.

## What worked

### Commandos: Behind Enemy Lines

GameSpot praised its specialist commandos, readable patrol/vision information, distinct environments and missions, and the satisfaction of building a plan and watching a synchronized operation work. The official page confirms six commandos with unique skills, sabotage objectives, 20 missions and cooperative play. The core success is **legible tactical problem-solving**: the player can understand the patrol, identify the specialist interaction and deliberately execute a plan.

### Partisans 1941

PC Gamer found it more flexible than the stricter Commandos formula. Players can combine stealth, ambushes, distractions and direct attacks; tactical pause and open maps support experimentation. The official page adds a hidden camp, squad recruitment, resources, character abilities and multiple ways to complete missions. Its strongest contribution is **a forgiving, systems-driven survival fantasy** rather than a single correct solution.

The base layer also gives missions a reason to matter: supplies, recruitment and preparation affect later operations. This is useful when it creates meaningful trade-offs, not just a shopping list.

### Broken Lines

Its strongest ideas are character and consequence. The game uses a stranded squad, morale, permadeath, branching mission choices and multiple campaign endings. Reviews that liked it repeatedly mention the personal story, ethical choices and replay value. The official page confirms eight soldiers, a fight-home premise, simultaneous action phases and story-changing choices.

Its lesson is that **being lost becomes emotionally valuable when the squad’s identity and decisions change the route home**, not merely when the map is large.

## What failed or weakened them

### Commandos: Behind Enemy Lines

The same precision that creates tension can create frustration. Contemporary and player reviews point to extreme difficulty, exact timing, puzzle-like solutions and pathing/control problems. Because failure often means repeating a long sequence, a mistake can feel like execution failure rather than an interesting consequence. The design is strong when patrol behaviour is predictable; it becomes harsh when the interface or timing obscures the reason for failure.

### Partisans 1941

Reviews consistently identify implementation friction: fiddly orders and inventory, enemy AI that can become implausibly omniscient, characters taking bad cover, grenades misbehaving, unclear alarm propagation and repetitive looting. The result is a good tactical core surrounded by chores and uncertainty. Its open-ended combat is valuable only when players can predict the consequences of their choices.

The useful warning is **do not confuse more systems with more agency**. Resources, camp upgrades and abilities must reduce decision friction, not add maintenance work between missions.

### Broken Lines

Its character/choice layer is stronger than its battlefield layer. Critics and players describe slow sections, limited mission variety, repetitive “advance and kill” encounters, weak or inconsistent AI, contrived combat, tutorial/presentation problems and bugs/optimization issues. Some reviews also found the relationship system too superficial to make the squad feel as personal as intended.

The warning is **story consequence cannot rescue repetitive encounters**. The return journey must be mechanically different from mission to mission, and the player must feel the consequences during play rather than only in dialogue or an ending screen.

## Cross-game findings

1. **The return journey needs a changing situation.** “Get back to base” is only compelling when routes, patrols, supplies, injuries, allies and objectives alter the next decision.
2. **Failure must be readable.** Commandos demonstrates the value of predictable enemies; Partisans demonstrates how bad AI/unclear detection can turn tension into irritation.
3. **Resources should create dilemmas, not chores.** Partisans’ camp and supplies are valuable when they shape preparation, but looting and inventory become drag when they are mandatory repetition.
4. **A small cast needs strong identity.** Broken Lines’ best material comes from individual soldiers, morale and choices; shallow relationship events do not create the same effect.
5. **Mission variety must be structural.** Different locations with the same “kill everyone and reach the marker” pattern will feel repetitive even with a compelling war story.
6. **A distinct camera or RTS label is insufficient.** The quality differentiator is the relationship between isolation, navigation, tactical decisions and consequences—not the fact that the setting is WWII.

## Limits

This is desk research from official pages, Steam aggregates and published reviews; no hands-on playtest was performed. Steam scores are current snapshots, not controlled measures of quality. Unreleased projects are excluded from good/bad judgments.
