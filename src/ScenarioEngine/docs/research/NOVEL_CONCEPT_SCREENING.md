## Strongest unresolved lead: an advance that consumes its own ground

Working description, not an approved game design: **a WWII combat RTS in which both armies' movement progressively changes how much traffic each route can carry.**

The precise hypothesis is not simply mud, destructible terrain, road construction or engineers repairing a bridge. It is a competitive feedback loop: committing heavy force damages a useful approach; the resulting loss of mobility constrains supply, recovery and retreat; engineers and lighter forces can preserve or restore alternatives; the opponent can exploit the routes your own advance has worn out.

**Verdict:** no direct match for the narrowly stated full loop was located in this pass. Its ingredients and several important interactions already exist. It is a research lead with moderate overlap risk, not a claim of a first-ever mechanic. Missing mentions in these pages are not evidence that a game lacks a feature. Further mod/older-game coverage remains incomplete.

### Concrete battle example — proposed behaviour

You capture a village with heavy armour. Repeated crossings churn the short field approach; towing a captured tank across it could strand the recovery vehicle and block the ammunition convoy. Meanwhile the enemy threatens the paved road.

You can send lighter units across another approach, secure and reinforce a new corridor, sacrifice recovery to keep the advance moving, or fight for the road. The same worn corridor may delay an enemy counterattack, but it can also trap your retreat. Salvage matters because recovering one vehicle uses ground capacity and engineer time needed elsewhere.

This example is a design hypothesis, not a claim about any existing game or historical event.

### Why it might be fun

- Army composition changes the map's future, giving heavy armour a visible strategic cost beyond purchase price.
- The opponent and your own decisions create new problems during play; there is no authored correct route to discover.
- Engineers create opportunities for manoeuvre while combat decides whether those opportunities can be used.
- Success can produce a clear player story: a lighter column reaches a flank that the heavier enemy can no longer cross.

These are design inferences. There is no evidence yet that they produce enjoyment, Steam demand or sufficient differentiation.

### Reject it if these conditions cannot be met

1. Route damage must be predictable and legible before committing units. Random bogging and unexplained pathfinding failures cannot carry the identity.
2. Players must issue route priorities and engineering goals at army scale. Repeatedly rescuing individual stuck trucks would turn strategy into maintenance work.
3. Several useful responses must exist. If every problem means placing the same repair action on the same spot, the result is the puzzle/cleanup loop Fahad rejected.
4. Combat, manoeuvre and the opponent must remain consequential. If removing enemies leaves substantially the same game, the concept is too close to an infrastructure simulator.
5. Comparative footage/manual/mod research must check whether another game already centres this exact feedback loop. A direct match would remove it from the novelty shortlist.
6. If ever authorised for a prototype, compare it against ordinary static terrain using the same map and forces. Retain it only if it creates different decisions and players describe those decisions as enjoyable. No prototype is authorised or started by this document.

## Search coverage and limits

Broad discovery covered deception, time loops, rebuilding, civil defence, card/deck tactics, asynchronous raids, convoy engineering and terrain manipulation. Only sufficiently verified findings are recorded above. Focused searches included `strategy game vehicles traffic soil mud deformation roads SnowRunner strategy`, `DORF RTS terrain roads deformation logistics official`, `Combat Mission bogging ground vehicles traffic terrain manual`, and `Foxhole mud vehicle road trench terrain degradation`, with follow-up official developer/publisher pages.

Some exact-phrase searches returned mostly irrelevant results. They provide no useful absence evidence. A generated-looking Gates of Hell aggregation mentioning rumoured traffic-responsive mud was excluded because it lacked primary substantiation. Combat Mission discovery material confirms that bogging is a relevant comparison, but this pass did not verify a current official manual establishing cumulative traffic wear. Neither exclusion supports claiming that feature absent.

No market-size, sales, player-review sample or technical-feasibility claim is made. Godot/art production remains deferred until a direction is selected; this research changes no engine code or assets.
