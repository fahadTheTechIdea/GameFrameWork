# Civilization-scale economy, politics and real-time warfare: precedent screen

Date: 24 October 2026. Status: research record; no design selected.

## Question screened

Does a game already combine Civilization-like nation development/economy and politics with real-time military play? Yes. The combination is an established strategy family, although different games put the real-time control at different levels.

## Closest precedents

| Game | What is real-time? | Economy / politics | WWII relevance | Distance from the proposed combination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Supreme Ruler Ultimate](https://store.steampowered.com/app/314980/supreme_ruler_ultimate?l=english) | Strategic and tactical military control in real time, with pause | Money/resources, trade, diplomacy, espionage, domestic/social policy and economic modernization | Direct: campaigns from WWII through the Cold War and future | Closest single-player WWII structural precedent; its emphasis is nation simulation and wargame control rather than Civilization-style city-by-city growth or hands-on Generals battles |
| [Knights of Honor II: Sovereign](https://store.steampowered.com/app/736820/Knights_of_Honor_II_Sovereign/) | Continuous grand-strategy map and direct RTS field battles | Province development, goods, income, trade, royal court roles, diplomacy, religion, espionage | No; medieval Europe | Closest “empire management plus directly commanded RTS battles” precedent |
| [Rise of Nations: Extended Edition](https://store.steampowered.com/app/287450/Rise_of_Nations_Extended_Edition/) | Conventional RTS battles and construction | Gather resources, build infrastructure, research, expand from an ancient city through the information age | Historical eras include WWII-era units, but not a WWII political simulation | Civilization-like technological progression inside a familiar RTS; politics is shallow compared with the proposal |
| [Total War](https://academy.totalwar.com/campaign-diplomacy/) | Battles are real-time, but the campaign layer is turn-based | Cities, armies, construction, trade and diplomacy | Several historical WWII-adjacent periods, but not a standard WWII mainline setting | Strong empire/battle precedent, but not continuous real-time nation management |
| [Imperix](https://imperix.io/) | Persistent real-time war fronts | Player-run companies, production chains, live market, elections, congress and laws | Contemporary geopolitical setting; browser MMO, not WWII | Very direct economy + politics + real-time war precedent, but every nation/system is player-driven and battles are not a conventional single-player RTS |
| [Hearts of Iron IV](https://store.steampowered.com/app/394360/Hearts_of_Iron_IV/) | Real-time-with-pause strategic war | WWII nation leadership, diplomacy and military/economic strategy | Direct WWII | Major WWII grand-strategy precedent; it does not provide Generals-style direct battlefield RTS control |

## Conclusion

The proposed structure is not distinct by itself. In particular, **Supreme Ruler Ultimate already covers the strongest WWII overlap**, while Knights of Honor II and Total War establish the empire-management-plus-real-time-battles pattern. Adding a more detailed economy or parliament to a WWII RTS would be an expansion of an existing strategy family, not a new whole-game identity.

The important distinction is the control boundary: Supreme Ruler/Hearts of Iron operate mainly at nation and operational scale; Knights of Honor II/Total War shift into direct battles; Rise of Nations keeps economy, construction and combat in one RTS layer; Imperix fuses economy, politics and war through a persistent player-run MMO. That is useful for comparison, but none of the differences should be treated as proof of originality.

No game was played hands-on in this pass. Claims are limited to official store, developer and publisher descriptions; absence of an exact match is not proof that none exists.
