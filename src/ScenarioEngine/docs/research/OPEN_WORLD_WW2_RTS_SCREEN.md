# Owner proposal: open-world WWII RTS with resource gathering and construction

Date: 24 October 2026. Status: rejected by owner as insufficiently distinct; retained as research history only.

Fahad asked about an open-world WWII game with resources and building like Command & Conquer: Generals. Working interpretation: top-down army command and a persistent playable region with resource collection, base construction and production. Persistence, single-player/co-op, world scale and economic detail remain assumptions, not decisions. The user's earlier no-duplicate requirement remains active.

## Initial competitor checks

- [Cepheus Protocol official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/979640/Cepheus_Protocol/) explicitly describes an open-world military RTS, with army command, large maps and a pandemic survival setting. It is Early Access; the page distinguishes current, experimental, legacy and planned systems. This is a structural precedent for open-world military RTS, not a WWII equivalent. Exact current economy/construction parity with Generals was not established in this pass.
- [Foxhole official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/505460/Foxhole/) documents persistent warfare, player-made supplies, base building, logistics and territory. [Developer FAQ](https://www.foxholegame.com/faq) expressly says the setting is fictional, not WWI or WWII. Every soldier is a player, so its individual-player MMO structure differs materially from one player commanding an RTS army.
- [The Longest Front creator page](https://desertfoxdev.itch.io/longestfront) documents a WWII shooter with base construction, recruitable/commandable soldiers and random missions. This establishes a related WWII building-and-command experience. It does not establish a seamless persistent RTS economy; no download was played.
- [Easy Red 2 developer site](https://www.easyred2.com/) describes WWII open-world battle maps, squad command and combined arms, but presents a shooter. Large battle maps do not by themselves establish the persistent construction/economy loop under consideration.

## Assessment

Owner decision: reject this direction. The open-world army-command structure plus resource gathering, base construction and production reads as a familiar RTS framework. A WWII setting and persistent map do not, by themselves, create the distinct whole-game identity being sought. Do not revive this proposal by renaming the same loop or adding isolated mechanics.

This is a coherent game structure worth evaluating on its merits, but the broad combination cannot be labelled without precedent. No exact historical-WWII, Generals-style, persistent-world match was verified in this initial screen. That limited result does not prove one absent. Searches also covered announced projects and smaller creator-hosted games, but were not exhaustive.

The meaningful open design question is whether the world is one ongoing strategic situation: bases, production and territorial losses persist as the army moves between regions; enemy forces can contest resources and counterattack without a new mission reset. A very large skirmish map or a campaign menu connecting separate battles offers a different experience.

Possible fun hypothesis: scout an industrial region, choose a defensible first base, establish production, build an army, seize another area and decide whether to hold or abandon earlier investments. The expanding front creates a choice between concentration and overextension. This describes the owner's idea concretely; it is not a newly verified original mechanic or proof of demand.

Primary risks to test after concept selection: repetitive territory clearing, excessive defence chores, unreadable off-screen attacks, and an economy that snowballs until decisions disappear. Keep resource and production decisions legible, in the accessible spirit the Generals comparison suggests. No code, art, feasibility benchmark or prototype was produced.
